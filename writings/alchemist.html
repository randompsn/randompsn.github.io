Hate-reviewing Paulo Coelho's "The Alchemist"

Merely reading the first line makes it clear that this review is filled with sincere sarcasm and hate towards the book, since what could be a better source to stimulate these two feelings of mine? If you're a fan, don’t ruin your day by reading this.

(Edit: seeing how popular this book is, gaining so many fans as if they’re in a cult, knowing it was on NYT bestseller #1 for like 687 weeks made me question what the hell has humanity evolved into, despite logically knowing these people most likely contribute little to the actual humanity development💀)

This is no doubt the worst book I’ve ever read. (It might not be your worst, given that I’m very picky on literature) I created a goodreads account just to give a 1-star review to it while searching for the original text to stimulate my sarcasm for creative writing.

It was shocking given how childish and linguistically easy to read it is; my old classmates in a bilingual school have read this in 6th grade. This book annoys me more than an actual fairytale since it came in disguised as a “philosophy of life” kind.

In a quick summary, the main problem is that the author's ton of “positive beliefs and values" are either not backed up or backed up with nonsensical rhetorics without going deeper. The text indeed holds a seemly deep tone but sarcastically the unbalanced depth between the essence and the tone just makes the latter so absurd like a “chuunibyou(中二病)” -- or “8th grade syndrome”, describing how 8th graders (of course not limited to actual 8th graders) might imagine themselves as “extraordinary”, “gifted”, “having hidden dark power” etc.

Here are some minor parts which also bugs me as hell when I was forced to read it in our high school English class. (what is happening with our education?)
-Way too clearly, the author’s sole conviction controlled everything, from the alchemist being able to shock enemies with using eye contact, a talking sun and a talking heart with zero foreshadowing given, to how Fatima’s “fragrance” was smelled by Santiago in the end when they’re literally thousands of miles apart, the list goes on. In real life, simply wishing something won’t make things as such happen, against the “When you want something the whole universe helps you achieve it”. What makes good things happen is logical thinking and effective execution, not daydreaming.
Some folks would argue that im too harsh for picking on the details, the book can be seen as purely entertainment and the overall idea is good, while criticizing me or similar comments for being “too skeptical” or something. Well, things like “life” which the book played isn’t to be played with, surely I don’t wish to see people doing that just because of dumbness. If the author really wanted to avoid all these comments, he could’ve just remove those “philosophical arguments”. I’m sure Mr.Coelho does this for some reason, might be his part of "personal legend" lol.
-I almost forgot, what the hell is the author’s intention of just repeating specific phrases like “personal legends”? I hope not just to sound cool.
Seriously, what’s bad of making breads? Fluffy, sweet and soft breads can make someone else’s day. Just because the bread maker used to want to travel makes him and his business to be blamed? Long before doing physics I was already especially into art, should I be blamed for doing physics then? I want to pursue both, and I can do both. The problem with stiffness of personal legends aside, is doing things non-related to personal legends a crime or what?
The problem addressed here is that rather than a generalized result that you can derive from carefully observing phenomenon, the author chooses to throw out some word and force characters (and readers) to agree and believe it. Why should we? Because yes. Now it sounds perfectly like cult to me=_=
By the way, even minerals are assigned with “personal legends” when they don't even qualify as being described by the word "person". As a sweaty STEM nerd I must say:
1) There’s no way to make copper and iron into the actual gold without undergoing nuclear fission reactions that alters the numbers of protons (the essential difference of each element, including the metallic elements such as copper, iron, gold etc., is it's proton number, not it’s color, not it’s stiffness, not its artificial marketing value), more specifically combining two nucleus to form a bigger one with more protons (gold has significantly more protons than the other two), which requires extremely high levels of temperature, pressure etc that are only satisfied inside stars or (very advanced) nuclear weapons. Indeed by mixing copper and zinc under moderatly high temperatures (around a few hundreds degree celcius) you’ll be able to get something gold-colored known as “brass” which is not The True Gold. Surely those alchemists secretly dislike gold lol.
2) Personifying elements and judging them by some rotten standards, implicating that copper and iron shouldn’t stay what they are or react naturally but to “turn into gold” is simply cringe. The author indeed values “nature” without knowing much about it, since the law of physics and chemistry leads to the difference in the proton numbers, and made way more copper & iron in our universe instead of gold. If ya’ll liked gold that much, why not replace all the hydrogen atoms (the most commonly-found element in the universe) in your body with gold?
-To me “Love in first sight” is cringe. People should really hold their worshiping of “love”, especially when the concept was abused as a tool for entertainment, controlling and of course, marketing. In an optimistic-er saying, spamming the concept of love just creates more disappointment and less clarity. In reality, you’ll most likely just fail the journey (fail to start your own company, for example) and never see Fatima again(now you don’t even have the money to book a flight lol), Fatima would be doom waiting, what a bad ending.
-Fatima is another useless women characters in such stories where they’re rather materialized and depicted as awards for the main character, or appeared as an idiotic insidious cheapskate which will eventually be defeated by the heroic masculinity. These features are common in Japanese & Chinese “isekai (alter-world) novels” which some folks sarcastically call “toilet paper”. What made this one worse? Pretending it’s the “philosophy of life” kind.
By the way, I didn’t even notice that Fatima’s “personal legend” isn’t mentioned at all, surely she’s designed to be so minor so that she won’t bother her men finishing his.

I never thought I would read such a book. The light novels I read in 6th grade (specifically, 86-the non exist region) are already orders of magnitudes deeper than it, but a cooked English literature class and some cooked evaluations which magically made this book bestseller of whatever list blows up my mind, so even if I usually don’t bother with such “adult fairytales” I ended up writing this long comment given how ridiculous all these things are going.
My same attitude with these self-helps goes to “Success books” which basically teach people how to succeed in life, mostly with nonsensical ideas. Both are just, sometimes disguised, selling some rotten dreams and beliefs on the market, attempting to turn humans into blind believers, which are dumb, non-doubting, and thus domesticated and farmed by the hungry society who would weave the sweetest dreams. Rarely do those authors belong to the former, however given that this author wrote such a book (not only conceptually, the technical aspects are also very cooked) he might be an exception. I also feel both laughable and pitiful for those who actually take it, as they either end up disillusioned by reality or continue to the doomed belief no matter what “obstacles”//“fact” arise.

Personally, I would choose thinking over kindness if I can only choose one, as kindness can always be regained when you’re still alive, while abandoning thinking is leaving the decision of your life to others. Since now, I’m always thankful of how I never give up thinking when making important decisions, which always gives me enough confidence to argue against my dad’s “suggestions” on a whim, and time proved me correct (despite logic already did).
Quite the contrary to the tone of this comment, I’m still a kind person, passionate about some things, probably didn’t believe in the utopia they weaved anymore, but have faith in my mind and hands which can make myself & world better, and dedicated probably more than Mr.Coelho to my own dream of learning physics & enjoying life as much as possible. What gives me the “power” is precisely questioning against believing. I often like to compare this book to my favorite movie “3 idiots”, which also expresses positive beliefs and values. However, the latter comes with carefully crafted narrative, character developments and sufficient depth. The takeaway is that exporting positive energy is and should be encouraged, but failing to portray it with a clear and critical mind against blinded faithfulness is misleading.
